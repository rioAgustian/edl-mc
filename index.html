Electric Double Layer −+ Monte Carlo

A positively charged electrode sits at x = 0 in a 1:1 salt solution. The electrolyte is cut into 80 thin planes; ions hop between neighbouring planes and each hop is accepted or rejected with the Metropolis rule. Watch the counterions pile up at the electrode and compare the averaged profiles with Gouy-Chapman theory.

Charged planes

Ions are smeared over planes of area A, so only the distance from the electrode matters. The field of an infinite charged plane is constant, so the energy of two planes grows linearly with their separation:

U = −Q₁Q₂·|x₁ − x₂| / (2εε₀A)

One trial move

  1. Pick one ion at random (a counterion, then a co-ion).
  2. Propose a hop to the plane on the left or right; hops through the electrode or the far wall are rejected.
  3. Compute ΔU from that ion’s interaction with the electrode and all planes.
  4. Accept if ΔU ≤ 0, otherwise accept with probability e−ΔU/kBT.

Things to try

  1. Raise T: the layer gets thicker and the linearised PB curve catches up with full PB.
  2. Raise the salt: λD shrinks as 1/√c₀.
  3. Set σ = 0: no double layer, flat profiles, φ = 0.
  4. Large σ: linearised PB predicts negative c⁺, which is unphysical.
